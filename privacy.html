PRIVACY

Privacy policy

LAST UPDATED · 25 MAY 2026

In one line

Your personal content — settings, bookmarks, what you read — stays on your device. Two anonymous operational pings to Expo Application Services (for over-the-air bug-fix delivery and aggregate launch counts) leave the device on every launch. On Android only, your coordinates or typed city also leave the device for a single OS-level geocoder lookup via Google Play Services when you set your location — iOS performs the equivalent lookup entirely on-device. No name, no email, no advertising identifiers, no third-party analytics, no sale of data to anyone. The full disclosure is below.

What we do not collect

  • No name, email address, phone number, or any other personally identifying information.
  • No user accounts, sign-in, or profile.
  • No advertising identifier (IDFA on iOS, AAID on Android — explicitly blocked in the manifest).
  • No record of what you read, what verses you bookmark, what city you set, or which calculation method you use is transmitted off your device. (Those preferences and your last-read position live in a local SQLite file the app uses to remember where you were — they are never sent anywhere, and you can delete them all by uninstalling the app.)
  • No tracking across other apps or websites. No retargeting. No data sold to anyone.
  • No microphone, camera, contacts, photo library, calendar, or health data accessed at any point.

What we do collect

Awaab uses Expo Application Services — the platform we build and ship the app with — for two narrow operational purposes. Both are required for the app to function in production (delivering bug fixes and prioritising development based on real-world adoption signal); using the app constitutes acceptance of this operational telemetry, and there is no in-app opt-out. The data is non-PII, non-tracking, and the entire scope is described below:

  • expo-updates — over-the-air bug fix delivery. On launch, the app asks Expo whether a JavaScript bundle update is available for your installed app version. The request carries: your app version, runtime version, OS version, and an anonymous Expo install ID. Endpoint: u.expo.dev. Without this, security and correctness fixes between binary releases would be undeliverable.
  • expo-insights — anonymous aggregate launch counts. Records that the app was opened on a device, so we can see how many users are on each shipped version and prioritize bug fixes for the versions in real use. The ping carries: your app version, runtime version, OS version, and the same anonymous Expo install ID. No screens visited, no taps, no search queries, no per-user behavior. Endpoint: i.expo.dev. Without this, post-launch fix prioritisation would be blind to which versions are actually in user hands.

The Expo install ID is randomly generated on first launch — not tied to your Apple/Google account, not an advertising identifier (IDFA/AAID), not derived from any hardware identifier. It is reset every time you uninstall and reinstall the app. No personal data is sent in either ping. Expo Inc. is the data processor; their privacy practices are at expo.dev/privacy. We do not sell, share, or otherwise hand this data to advertisers, brokers, or any other party.

EU/GDPR users — legal basis: the lawful basis for processing this operational telemetry is legitimate interest under Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. The processing is necessary for the app to be supportable in production (without it we cannot ship targeted bug fixes or prioritise development against real adoption signal), the data carries no personal identifiers and no tracking surface, and the privacy intrusion is minimal. We assessed that the legitimate interest is not outweighed by user rights given those conditions. If you would prefer that none of this telemetry leaves your device, uninstalling the app removes the install ID and stops every future ping — Awaab is fully usable for as long as it is installed, and walking away leaves no residue.

What is stored on your device

The following data lives only on your device, and you can delete it by uninstalling the app:

  • Settings — language, theme, calculation method, Asr school, time format, prayer-alert toggles, and Arabic font size.
  • Location coordinates — when granted, used only to compute prayer times. Stored only on your device; on Android, they leave the device once for the OS-level geocoder lookup described under Network traffic (to derive your city name). Awaab itself does not store or transmit them.
  • City label — derived from your coordinates via the OS geocoder (on iOS: Apple's on-device CLGeocoder; on Android: Google Play Services — see Network traffic), or whatever city you manually entered.
  • Last-read position — the sūrah and verse you were reading, so you can resume.
  • Bookmarks — verses you have saved.
  • Cached prayer times — pre-computed for your location, refreshed daily on-device.

None of this is shared with us, with Apple or Google beyond what the OS already does for any installed app, or with anyone else.

Network traffic

The complete list of network requests Awaab makes:

  • u.expo.dev (Expo Updates) — checked on cold launch and at most once per session, to fetch over-the-air JavaScript fixes for your installed app version. Anonymous install ID + app/runtime/OS version only.
  • i.expo.dev (Expo Insights) — anonymous launch ping. Same install ID + version triplet. No screens, no taps, no per-user behavior.
  • Google Play Services geocoder (Android only) — when you grant location permission, Android delegates the coordinate-to-city lookup to Google Play Services, and your coordinates leave the device once for that single OS-level lookup. The same delegation happens if you type a city name manually — the typed string is sent to Google for resolution. On iOS, the equivalent lookup happens entirely on-device via Apple's CLGeocoder and never reaches a server. Awaab does not store or transmit either the query or the response; the request originates at the OS level. Google's privacy policy applies: policies.google.com/privacy.
  • App Store / Play Store (OS-level) — the operating system itself may check for binary updates. We do not control this; it is the same behavior as any other installed app.

That is the complete list. Awaab does not contact ad networks, third-party analytics services, third-party crash reporters, social-media SDKs, or any server beyond those listed above.

Third-party services

Awaab uses Expo Application Services (operated by Expo Inc.) as its primary third-party processor, for the two purposes described above (expo-updates + expo-insights). Their privacy policy is at expo.dev/privacy. On Android, the OS-level reverse-geocoder is provided by Google Play Services; when you grant location permission or type a city name, that single OS-level lookup is processed by Google under their privacy policy. Awaab transmits no data to any other third party.

The marketing site at elshazlyyy.github.io/Awaab-Public uses no analytics, no cookies, and no third-party scripts. Fonts are self-hosted from this site, not loaded from any third-party CDN. Browsing these pages contacts no server other than GitHub Pages, which serves the static files.

Data deletion

Because no data on our side is linked to you personally, deletion happens automatically:

  • On your device — uninstalling Awaab removes every setting, bookmark, last-read position, and cached prayer time. There is no cloud account to delete because there is no cloud account.
  • The anonymous Expo install ID is randomly generated locally on first launch. Uninstalling the app removes it; a future reinstall generates a fresh, unrelated ID. Expo retains the aggregated, non-personally-identifiable launch counts only.
  • If you would like Expo to investigate or remove any specific record from their backend, contact them at expo.dev/privacy with the date range; we are also happy to help — email us and we will assist.

Children's privacy

Awaab is rated 4+ on the App Store and Everyone on Google Play, and is suitable for all ages. The anonymous operational telemetry described above contains no personally identifying information about anyone, regardless of age. We do not knowingly target the app at children under 13, nor do we collect any information that could identify a child. The app is therefore compliant with COPPA (US), the GDPR's standards for children (EU/UK), and the equivalent provisions in other jurisdictions.

Changes to this policy

If we ever change what data the app handles, we will update the date at the top of this page and ship the new policy with the corresponding app release. We will not change this policy silently.
