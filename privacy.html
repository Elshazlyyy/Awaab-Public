Privacy Policy

Your data stays with you.

Last updated: May 21, 2026

Awaab is designed so that you never have to trust us with your information — because we never receive any. This page explains exactly what happens, in plain language.

The short version

  • Awaab collects, stores, and transmits no personal data.
  • No account is required to use the app.
  • No analytics, no advertising, no third-party trackers, no crash-reporting services.
  • Location and motion sensors are used only on your device, and only for the features that need them.
  • All Quran content and prayer-time calculations are bundled with the app and run on-device.

What Awaab does not collect

Awaab does not collect, store, or transmit personal data. There is no account system, no sign-in, and no identifier that ties the app to you. We do not run analytics, we do not show advertising, we do not use any third-party tracking SDK, and we do not use any crash-reporting service. The app's source code contains zero remote URLs and zero network calls of its own.

Where your data lives

Anything Awaab needs to remember — your preferences, your bookmarks, your selected calculation method, your last known location — is written to a local SQLite database file on your device. It is never copied to a server, because Awaab does not operate any server.

On-device sensors

Location

If you grant location permission, your device's location is used only on your device to calculate accurate prayer times for where you are, and to determine the bearing toward the qibla. Awaab itself never transmits or stores your location off your device.

One honest nuance about reverse geocoding. When you ask Awaab to detect your location, the app may also ask your operating system to translate your coordinates into a city name to display ("Mecca, Saudi Arabia"). On iOS and Android, this lookup is handled by Apple's or Google's own geocoding services. That step is performed by the OS, not by Awaab, and is governed by their own privacy policies. Awaab cannot see or capture what the OS does with that request.

Compass and motion sensor

The compass and motion sensors are used only on your device to power the qibla finder. Heading data stays on the device — it is not transmitted anywhere.

Quran and prayer-time content

Quran text and prayer-time calculations are fully local. The Quran content ships inside the app as a bundled SQLite database (~2.6 MB) and is read directly from your device's storage. Prayer times are computed on your device using a standard astronomical formula and your location. The app does not make network requests to load Quran content or to fetch prayer times.

Notifications

Awaab can schedule local notifications to remind you when each prayer time enters. These notifications are scheduled in advance, in a rolling window, and are delivered entirely by your device's operating system. They are not push notifications from a server — there is no server.

What Apple and Google may collect

When you download or use Awaab through the App Store or Google Play, the platforms themselves may collect standard platform-level information — for example, download counts, device model, operating-system diagnostics, and crash reports they collect on Awaab's behalf at the OS level. This data is governed by Apple's and Google's own privacy policies and is outside Awaab's control. Awaab does not access this data and does not have any analytics agreement with either platform.

How Awaab is built

Awaab is built with Expo and React Native — standard, well-known cross-platform frameworks. These provide the runtime that draws the screens; they do not add tracking, telemetry, or remote logging of their own to the production app. No additional analytics or tracking SDK is included in Awaab.

Children

Because Awaab does not collect personal information from anyone, it does not collect personal information from children. The app is safe to use at any age — see the Age Suitability statement for more.

Changes to this policy

If anything about how Awaab handles data changes, this page will be updated and the "last updated" date above will change to reflect it. The privacy posture above is a core design commitment of the app — we don't intend to change it.
